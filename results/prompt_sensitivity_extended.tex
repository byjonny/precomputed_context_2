\paragraph{Prompt sensitivity of the composition effects.}
The composition effects (1--2~pp) are small next to the insight effect
($\sim$9~pp), so we test their robustness to prompt phrasing. We run the full
grid under two headers that differ \emph{only} in the hint-usage instruction,
holding everything else fixed (same 1{,}000 items, seed~42, temperature~0.7,
condition mapping). The \textbf{directive} prompt explicitly tells the model to
evaluate and exploit the hint, whereas the softer \textbf{TRS} prompt used
elsewhere marks the hints as optional. Crucially, overall accuracy is unchanged
by the swap: the mean $\{q,i\}$ baseline is $53.6\%$ under both prompts
(Table~\ref{tab:promptsens}, first columns). Any divergence that follows
therefore reflects a change in composition \emph{structure}, not in raw
capability.

Table~\ref{tab:promptsens} reports each effect under both headers, and the two
effect types behave very differently. \emph{Repetition is prompt-robust}: the
problem-first ($\{q,i{\mid}q,i\}-\{q,i\}$) and insight-first
($\{i,q{\mid}i,q\}-\{i,q\}$) increments stay positive and significant when
pooled under either header. \emph{Ordering is not}: problem-first beats
insight-first by a pooled $+2.0$~pp ($p<.001$) under the directive prompt, but
by only $+0.8$~pp (n.s.) under TRS.

Two factors explain the fragility of the ordering effect. First, the
$\{q,i\}>\{i,q\}$ advantage is engagement-mediated: it arises from integrating
the hint with an already-encoded problem, and a stronger usage directive
amplifies that integration. Softening the instruction removes the amplifier.
Second, the effect is fragile even under the directive prompt, where it is
carried largely by Qwen2.5-7B ($+5.3$~pp, the only model significant at
$p<.01$); the remaining models are marginal ($+2.3$ to $+2.7$~pp,
$p=.04$--$.07$), although differences do exist and appear to favor the directive
prompt. At temperature~0.7, where single cells carry sampling noise comparable
to these effect sizes, such borderline effects could regress toward zero in a
fresh run.

The direction of the shift is nonetheless consistent: for all seven models the
ordering delta is larger under the directive prompt than under TRS
(Table~\ref{tab:promptsens}; sign test $p<.02$). A uniform shift across every
model is unlikely under sampling noise alone, which indicates a genuine effect
of the prompt and matches the engagement-mediated account above, where the
stronger directive systematically widens the problem-first advantage. We
therefore treat ordering as a second-order, prompt-conditional effect that is
most pronounced under the directive prompt and robust only in Qwen2.5-7B, while
repetition holds across prompts.
